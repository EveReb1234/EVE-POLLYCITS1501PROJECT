What the data shows for the Noongar region

  • Noongar was the most commonly spoken Aboriginal language in both areas in both years. In 2016 it was spoken by 0.9% of people in Perth and 0.6% in South-Western WA. By 2021 it was 2.3% in both.
  • Use of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander languages at home rose between 2016 and 2021: from 2.3% to 4.4% in Perth and from 1.6% to 3.9% in South-Western WA.
  • English is still the main language at home, but its share fell: from 88.8% to 85.8% in Perth and from 92.8% to 89.4% in South-Western WA.

About the data

Source: Australian Bureau of Statistics, Language Statistics for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples, 2021 (Tables 1.5 and 2.5, Western Australia). Figures are the percentage of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people in each region, worked out from the counts in the spreadsheet.

People can speak more than one language at home, so the percentages for a region add up to more than 100%. "Not stated" responses are left out of the charts. The Noongar region is shown using two ABS Indigenous Regions, Perth and South-Western WA. ABS boundaries do not match Noongar boundaries exactly, so some places on the edge of Noongar country, such as the Esperance area, fall in a different ABS region and are not included. Small random adjustments are applied by the ABS to protect privacy.
